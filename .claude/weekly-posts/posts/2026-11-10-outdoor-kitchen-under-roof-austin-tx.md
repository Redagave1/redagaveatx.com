## Why Put an Outdoor Kitchen Under a Roof?

A roof turns a grill station into a room. It keeps the cook and the counters out of the afternoon sun, and it keeps rain off the appliances, the countertop, and whoever is standing at it. Austin rain tends to arrive all at once: the City of Austin describes the region as Flash Flood Alley, and normal rainfall at Camp Mabry is 36.25 inches a year.

A roof also gives you a ceiling, and the ceiling is where the best parts of an outdoor kitchen hang: recessed lights over the counter, a ceiling fan to keep the air moving on a still summer evening, and the wiring for both, out of sight above the boards. ## What Should You Settle Before the Counter Goes In?

The hardest parts of an outdoor kitchen to change are the ones you stop seeing once it is built. Settle these on the plan first:

- **Where the gas, water, and power come from.** Each line runs under the slab or inside the base, so its route is fixed before anything is poured or framed.
- **What hangs from the ceiling.** Lights, a fan, and a TV each need their wiring in place before the ceiling boards close.
- **Where the outlets go.** Along the back wall, above the counter, where a blender or a slow cooker will actually sit.
- **What goes under the counter.** Refrigerators, drawers, and access doors each take a set width in the base, so choose the appliances before the base is built.
- **What is underfoot.** The floor sets the look of the whole room, from wood-look tile to poured concrete.
