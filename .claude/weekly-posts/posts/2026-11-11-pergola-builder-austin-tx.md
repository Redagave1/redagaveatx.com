## Should You Build a New Pergola or Rebuild the Old One?

Start with what is in the yard now.

- **Build new** when there is no shade where you sit. In Cedar Park we built the pergola from scratch.
- **Rebuild** when a pergola already stands where you want the shade. The Circle C pergola was sun-bleached and failing. We kept the structure, reframed it, and gave it a new steel top.

The two routes lead to different work. A new pergola starts with posts and beams, so its shape is open: long and narrow, square, attached to the house, or standing free. A rebuild keeps the footprint that is already there, and the design work goes into the frame and the top.

## Why Leave the Steel to Rust?

Because the homeowners wanted it. They asked for steel that rusts, so we left the frame to weather to a deep brown patina.

The color is the reason. Rusted steel sits between the warm wood of the fence and rafters and the pale stone of the patio, and it ties the two together.

Rust is a finish you choose. The other route is powder coat, and we powder-coat steel pergolas in any color. ## Which Way Should the Rafters Run?

Across the sun. A rafter casts shade when the sun crosses it, and it casts almost none when the sun runs along it.

- **Rafters that run east to west** block the high midday sun, which comes from the south
- **Rafters that run north to south** block the morning and late-afternoon sun
- **Close spacing** blocks more sun in either direction

On the Cedar Park pergola the rafters are wide boards set close together, and they cross the short way, from one beam to the other.

Low sun is a separate problem. Late in the day it comes in under any pergola, so shade at that hour comes from the side: a fence, a screen, or planting. In Cedar Park the horizontal privacy fence closes one long side of the pergola for its full length.

## Does a Pergola Need a Permit in Austin?

Size and attachment decide it. The City of Austin's Development Services Department lists the work that is exempt from a building permit. For a home, that list includes a one-story detached accessory structure that is no more than 200 square feet and no more than 15 feet tall, has no plumbing, does not create a dwelling, and sits outside a flood hazard area. The department also notes that exempt work still has to meet the building code and city code.

A pergola attached to the house is not a detached structure, so that exemption does not cover it.

Cedar Park and the other cities around Austin have their own rules. Many neighborhoods review exterior changes too, and there a pergola goes to the committee as a drawn plan before we build. We handle the permit and the plans. Our [patio cover permit guide](patio-cover-permit-austin-tx) covers the details for Austin.
