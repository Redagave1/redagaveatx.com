**Red Agave Landscape designed and built a round water feature in a Southeast Austin backyard that is constructed like a small swimming pool: a concrete shell, a plaster interior, and a sand pool filter.** Water comes out of a hole drilled in a boulder, runs down a steel channel, and falls into the pool. A floating steel fire pit curves along the front, a second fire pit is suspended between two boulders behind it, and a steel planter welded on site holds a tree. Our own crew built it in spring 2025.

## Why Build a Water Feature Like a Pool?

Because this one is a pool. It is small, round, and raised above the patio, and it is built from the same three parts as a swimming pool:

- **Concrete shell:** a round concrete wall, formed and poured in place
- **Plaster:** the finished surface inside the shell
- **Sand pool filter:** the water circulates through it, the same way the water in a swimming pool does

Each part has a job. The shell holds a true circle and carries the cap on its rim. The plaster gives the inside its finished surface. The filter keeps the water clear.

The inside is finished black, and that sets the look of the whole space. Black water works like a dark mirror. In the photo above, the surface reflects the flames in front of it and the evening sky above it.

## How Was It Built?

The build photos show the order of the work.

- **Dig and edge:** we excavated the round hole, set the steel edging, and placed the boulders
- **Form and pour:** the round wall was formed and poured in place, and then the forms came off
- **Plumb:** PVC lines run to the shell, with a fitting set through the wall
- **Finish:** the interior was plastered and finished black, and the outside of the wall was finished smooth
- **Fill and cap:** the pool was filled, and the dark cap was set on the rim in curved sections

The boulders went in early, at the same stage as the excavation. They are part of the structure of the design: one is the source of the water, and two more carry a fire pit.

The patio around the pool is concrete, poured as separate pads. The joints between the pads are filled with dark gravel, and one pad was formed on a curve to follow the round wall. See our [masonry and concrete](../services/masonry-concrete-austin-tx) work.

## Where Does the Water Come From?

From a boulder. The source is a boulder with a hole drilled in it. Water comes out of the boulder and into a steel channel, and the channel carries it to the pool, where it falls to the surface.

The channel is open, so you watch the water travel before it drops. The fall moves the black surface, and it is the sound of the space.

Everything else about the water is pool practice. The sand filter cleans it as it circulates, and the plaster shell holds it.

## What Are the Two Fire Pits?

Both are custom steel, and each one has its own shape.

- **The floating fire pit:** a curved steel fire pit that follows the front of the pool. It has a custom burner, and the flames run along the curve. It is set off the concrete, so it reads as floating.
- **The boulder fire pit:** a square steel pan with a ring burner, suspended between two boulders behind the pool.

Fire beside water is the point of the layout. The front fire pit sits at the foot of the pool wall, so the flames light the wall and reflect on the water.

## What Is the Steel Piece With the Tree?

It is a planter. A square steel tray sits on a tapered steel base. We cut the pieces from steel plate and welded them on site, then set the planter and checked it with a level.

The finished planter holds a young tree, river rock, and low plants. The tapered base has an open slot, and the slot is packed with river rock and lit from inside. At night the base glows.

The steel has weathered to a deep brown, the same tone as the floating fire pit.

## FAQ: Questions We Hear

### Is a water feature built like a pool really a pool?
Technically, yes. This one has a concrete shell, a plaster interior, and a sand pool filter. Those are the parts of a swimming pool, at a much smaller size.

### Can a fire pit go next to a water feature?
Yes. On this Southeast Austin project, a curved steel fire pit follows the front of the pool, and a second fire pit sits behind it. The flames reflect on the water.

### Can a fire pit be built in a custom shape?
Yes. The front fire pit on this project is curved to follow the round pool, and its burner is custom. The second is a square steel pan suspended between two boulders.

### Where does the water in this feature come from?
From a boulder with a hole drilled in it. A steel channel carries the water from the boulder to the pool.

### Do you weld steel on site?
Yes. The steel planter on this project was cut from steel plate and welded on site by our own crew.
