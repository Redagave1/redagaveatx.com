**Red Agave Landscape designs pools in Austin, TX as part of the whole yard, because what goes around the water decides how the pool gets used.** A rectangle of water is the easy part. The deck, the fire, the shade, and the way you walk to the pool from the house are where a pool design is won. Here are three plans drawn by the owner, and one pool we built in East Austin.

[[fig images/designs/pool-deck-fire-court-plan.webp | Pool design plan with a large pool and a small pool wrapped by a deck, a herringbone paver court with a linear fire pit, pale pads, and agaves in square planters || Plan 1. ## Plan 1: What Goes Next to the Pool?

A second place to be. On this plan the water is two rectangles side by side, one large and one small, wrapped by a deck. The plan gives just as much room to what is beside them:

- **A fire court:** herringbone pavers with a linear fire pit
- **Pads to the lawn:** pale pads that step from the court out to the grass
- **Agaves:** set in square planters along the way

A pool yard gets used on nights when no one swims, and the fire court is the reason to be out there.

## Plan 2: How Do You Fit a Pool on an Angled Lot?

Let the terrace take the angle. The pool stays a clean rectangle with a pale deck around it. Beside it, a terrace of square pavers is turned on the diagonal to run with the long edge of the yard.

[[fig images/designs/pool-paver-terrace-plan.webp | Pool design plan of a rectangular pool beside a terrace of square pavers set on the diagonal with green joints, a tiled fire pit, and a deck along the house || Plan 2. A rectangular pool, and a paver terrace turned to follow the angle of the yard. The callouts enlarge the paver pattern.]]

The pavers have green joints, so the terrace reads as part lawn and part patio. A fire pit bordered in patterned blue tile sits at its center. Along the house, a deck holds a square of water and two planters.

## Plan 3: What Fits Beside a Pool on a Tight Lot?

One small room. This pool has a terracotta border on three sides and a deck with broad steps on the fourth. Off to one side is a small terracotta courtyard with one feature at its center.

[[fig images/designs/pool-courtyard-plan.webp | Pool design plan of a pool with a terracotta border, a deck with broad steps, and a small terracotta courtyard to one side || Plan 3. A pool, a stepped deck, and a courtyard in the same terracotta. The thin green lines point to material photos on the full sheet.]]

Using one material twice ties the pool and the courtyard together. The courtyard also gives the yard a place to sit that is away from the water.

## What Gets Decided First in a Pool Design?

Four things, before the shape of the water:

- **The height:** where the coping sits against the house floor and the deck
- **The walk:** how you get from the back door to the water
- **The shade:** where people sit on a summer afternoon
- **The water after a storm:** where rain goes once the yard is mostly hard surface

### Do you design the pool and the yard together?
Yes. The owner draws one plan for the pool, the deck, the fire, the planting, and the lighting, and our own crew builds it.

### Can a pool fit on a small or angled lot?
Yes. One of these plans turns a paver terrace on the diagonal to follow an angled yard, and another fits a pool, a stepped deck, and a courtyard on a tight lot.

### What should go around a pool?
A deck or coping at the right height, a shaded place to sit, and a second place to be, such as a fire court. Drainage for the hard surfaces is planned with them.
