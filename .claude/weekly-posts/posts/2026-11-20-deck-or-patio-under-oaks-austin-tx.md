**Under a protected oak in Austin, TX, build a deck near the tree and keep a patio to the outer part of the root zone.** A patio needs a dug and compacted base under every square foot. A deck touches the ground only at its footings. The City of Austin limits digging near regulated trees, so the tree decides the surface. Red Agave Landscape builds both, and this post shows the rules and the yards where we built around the trees.

## Which Oaks Are Protected in Austin?

Inside Austin city limits, any tree 19 inches or larger in diameter is protected and needs a City Arborist permit to remove. Oaks, pecans, elms, and other listed species at 24 inches and larger are heritage trees.

The City's page on [trees on residential property](https://www.austintexas.gov/development-services/trees-residential-property) gives the method: measure around the trunk 4.5 feet above the ground, then divide by 3.14. A 19-inch tree measures about 60 inches around. The same page says trees under 19 inches are not regulated on residential property.

The cities next to Austin write their own rules:

| City | Tree rule |
|---|---|
| Austin | Trees 19 inches and larger are protected. Listed species at 24 inches and larger are heritage trees. |
| [Rollingwood](../locations/rollingwood-tx) | A city permit for any tree trimming or removal. No oak trimming from February 1 through June 30. |
| [West Lake Hills](../locations/westlake-hills-tx) | Trees from 10 to 14 inches need a permit. Trees 14 inches and larger need a City Council variance and 150 percent replacement. Diameter is measured 54 inches up the trunk. |

## What Is the Critical Root Zone?

It is the ground around a regulated tree where the City of Austin limits disturbance, and its size follows the size of the trunk. The City's page uses a tree whose root zone reaches 20 feet from the base, and sets three limits inside it:

- **The inner quarter:** no impacts are allowed
- **The inner half:** no cut or fill greater than 4 inches
- **The full zone:** 50 percent of the ground must be preserved without impact

In that example, digging is allowed in the outer half, between 10 and 20 feet from the tree. The page also names what counts as an impact, including grade changes and soil compaction from construction equipment.

A project that disturbs the root zone of a regulated tree needs a Tree Ordinance Review Application. So does pruning 25 percent or more of the canopy.

## Why Does a Patio Ask More of the Ground Than a Deck?

Because of what is under it. Every patio we build sits on 6 to 8 inches of compacted crushed limestone, in ground that has been dug out and compacted first. That is more than the 4 inches of cut the City allows in the inner half of a root zone, and it covers the whole footprint of the patio.

A deck is different. Our deck frames are mild steel, on steel posts set in concrete footings. Each footing is one hole, and steel allows longer spans between posts, so a deck needs few of them. The footings go where the City allows digging, and none go in the inner quarter of a root zone. Most of the ground under a deck stays open to air and water.

| Compare | Deck | Patio |
|---|---|---|
| What touches the ground | Footings | The whole footprint |
| Digging | One hole at each footing | A dug and compacted base, 6 to 8 inches of crushed limestone |
| Ground under it | Open to air and water | Covered |
| Height | Set to the door, with no step | Follows the ground |

## When Does a Patio Still Work Under Trees?

When it stays out of the inner root zone and lets rain reach the soil. Three ways we build it:

- **Pour pads, not one slab.** Pads with gravel joints let rain soak in between them.
- **Use gravel.** A gravel court is a floor that drains. It needs an edge to stay where it is put, and we build that edge in steel.
- **Build over what is already there.** In a second Circle C yard we laid composite decking over an old flagstone patio, so the new deck sits on ground that was already covered. See [that project](../projects/hopeland).

## When Can Oaks Be Pruned for a Build?

Not in spring. The [Texas A&M Forest Service](https://tfsweb.tamu.edu/prevent-the-spread-of-oak-wilt-in-texas-this-spring) asks Texans to avoid pruning or wounding oaks from February through June, to slow the spread of oak wilt, and to paint every wound on an oak at any time of year.

Rollingwood writes the same window into its rules. Oaks there cannot be trimmed from February 1 through June 30, and any trimming needs a city permit.

So when a build in Rollingwood needs a limb trimmed, the trimming goes through the city's permit first, and oak pruning waits until after June 30. Anywhere in the Austin area, the months outside February through June are the time to prune an oak.

## FAQ: Questions We Hear

### Is a deck or a patio better under an oak tree?
A deck, near the tree. A deck touches the ground only at its footings, and most of the ground under it stays open to air and water. A patio needs a dug and compacted base under its whole footprint.

### What size tree is protected in Austin?
Any tree 19 inches or larger in diameter, measured 4.5 feet above the ground. Listed species at 24 inches and larger, including oaks, are heritage trees.

### How close to a protected tree can you dig in Austin?
The City of Austin allows no impacts in the inner quarter of the critical root zone and no cut or fill greater than 4 inches in the inner half. Half of the ground in the full zone must be preserved without impact.

### Can a patio be built under trees?
Yes, when it stays out of the inner root zone. We pour separate pads with gravel joints, use a gravel court edged in steel, or build over a surface that is already there.

### When should oaks not be pruned in Texas?
From February through June. The Texas A&M Forest Service asks Texans to avoid pruning or wounding oaks in those months, and to paint every wound on an oak at any time of year.
