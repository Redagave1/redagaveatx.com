Backyard Makeover in Austin, TX: Three Before and Afters

November 12, 2026

Red Agave Landscape designs and builds backyard makeovers in Austin, TX, and the best ones keep what is worth keeping. Here are three yards before and after: a bare slope in Circle C that became steel terraces, a failing pergola in Circle C that became a dining room, and a flat lawn in Southeast Austin that became a patio with water and fire. Each one was drawn by the owner and built by our own crew.

What Can You Do With a Bare Slope?

Cut it into levels. This Circle C backyard was bare ground under a live oak, and it dropped away from the house with nothing to hold it. None of it was level enough to use.

We cut the slope into five level planes: three steel terraces that step down through gravel and boulders, and two deck levels off the house. Quarter-inch steel edges every transition. A gravel court with two fire features sits between the decks and the terraces.

  • What stayed: the live oak. Lanterns now hang in its canopy.
  • What changed: every square foot of ground under it

See the full Circle C case study, and the plan this yard was built from in our post on landscape design build.

What Can You Do With a Failing Pergola?

Keep it and rebuild it. This second Circle C yard had a wood pergola that was sun-bleached and failing, over an old flagstone patio.

We kept the structure, reframed it, and capped it with custom-cut steel triangles. Composite decking now runs under it in one plane, laid over the old flagstone. Black pendants hang under the beams and light a dining table.

  • What stayed: the pergola frame and the flagstone under the deck
  • What changed: the top, the floor, and the light

The rest of the yard was built around it: a slatted cabana, a boulder fire pit, and steel planters lit from under their rims. See the Circle C project and our post on choosing a pergola builder.

The plan for that yard shows the same parts on paper: the decks, the deck steps, the fire pit, and concrete pavers cut on the diagonal, each field dimensioned. More of our drawings are on the design plans page.

What Can You Do With a Flat Lawn?

Give it a center. This Southeast Austin backyard was a flat lawn behind the house. The first photo is from the start of the build: a round hole, steel edging, and boulders set in place.

That hole became a round water feature built like a small pool, with a concrete shell, a plaster interior, and a sand pool filter. Water runs from a drilled boulder down a steel channel. A curved steel fire pit follows the pool wall, and a second fire pit hangs between two boulders. The patio is concrete pads with dark gravel joints.

  • What stayed: the lawn beyond the new patio
  • What changed: the corner of the yard by the house, which is now a patio with water and fire

See how this yard was built, and the plan next to the finished fire pits.

Do You Have to Tear Everything Out?

No. In all three yards, something stayed: a live oak, a pergola frame, a lawn. Keeping it is often the better design.

Trees are the clearest case. Inside Austin city limits, the City Arborist program protects trees with a diameter of 19 inches or more, so the layout works around them. In Circle C the live oak stayed, and the terraces step down beneath it.

What Comes First in a Makeover?

The plan. Every Red Agave project starts with a free consultation, and the CAD plan is included. We walk the yard with you, take measurements, and study sun, shade, drainage, soil, and existing trees. Design development takes 2 to 4 weeks, depending on scope.

Then the work goes in order: drainage first, then the hardscape that sets the levels, then planting and lighting. See our design and build service, more results on our before and after page, and our backyard renovation guide.

Questions We Hear

How do I start a backyard makeover in Austin?

With a free on-site consultation. We walk the yard with you and take measurements, and the owner draws the plan. The CAD plan is included.

Do you have to remove everything in the yard?

No. In these three makeovers we kept a live oak, a pergola frame, and a lawn. We build around what is worth keeping.

Can an old pergola be saved?

Yes. In Circle C we kept a failing wood pergola, reframed it, and capped it with custom-cut steel triangles.

Can a sloped backyard be made usable?

Yes. In Circle C we cut a bare slope into three steel terraces and two deck levels.

Who does the work?

Our own crew. Red Agave Landscape designs and builds every project.
