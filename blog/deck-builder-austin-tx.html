Deck Builder in Austin, TX: Set the Height Before You Choose the Wood

November 11, 2026

Red Agave Landscape is a deck builder in Austin, TX, and two of our decks show the decision to settle first: the height of the finished surface and what it meets. In East Austin we built a teak deck that runs flush from a glass-walled screened porch to an outdoor kitchen, along an inground pool. In Bee Cave we built a wood deck with steel cable railing that sits low on a Hill Country slope, beside steel-walled gravel terraces. Both were owner-designed and built by our own crew.

What Should You Look for in a Deck Builder in Austin?

Look for a builder who answers five questions before the first board is ordered, and who can show you a finished deck for each answer.

  • Height: how high is the finished surface, and what does it meet?
  • Structure: what is the frame made of?
  • Material: which board suits the sun?
  • Railing: does the deck need one, and what does it do to the view?
  • Surroundings: what happens where the deck ends?

On structure, our answer is steel. Red Agave builds deck frames in mild steel, with steel posts set in concrete footings. Steel does not warp, rot, or attract termites, so the frame outlasts the boards on top of it.

The other four are answered below on two decks we built: the East Austin project and the deck on our Bee Cave page. See also our custom decks service.

Why Does the Deck Height Come First?

The height decides whether you step up, step down, or walk straight out.

In East Austin the answer was to walk straight out. The deck runs flush from the screened porch to the kitchen, with no grade change and no transition strip. Someone carrying a plate from the grill to the table inside never takes a step up or down.

The same height carries to the water. The deck meets the stone coping of the pool, and from inside the porch the pool is framed like a picture.

In Bee Cave the lot slopes, and the answer was to keep the deck low. It sits close to the gravel under the live oaks, and a stair climbs from it to an upper level.

Height also decides the paperwork. Austin Development Services lists one kind of deck as exempt from a building permit: a deck of 200 square feet or less that is no more than 30 inches above grade at any point, is not attached to the house, is not a way out of the house, and is outside a flood hazard area. A deck that misses any one of those conditions needs a permit. On a slope the height is the catch: a deck that is low at one end can be well over 30 inches at the other. Our deck permit guide covers the process.

Which Decking Material Suits Austin Sun?

The East Austin deck is teak. We also build in IPE hardwood, Western red cedar, thermally modified wood, and composite.

In Austin the choice turns on sun and bare feet. Our guide to the best deck materials for Austin heat puts it in one line: shade matters more than the board. Composite needs no staining or sealing, and it runs hotter in direct sun than wood. IPE stays cooler than composite in the same sun, and it can be oiled each year to hold its brown or left to weather to silver.

The East Austin deck follows that rule. A red sail shade covers the kitchen and the seating end of the deck, so the places where people stand and sit are shaded.

What Railing Keeps a Hill Country View Open?

Cable railing. On the Bee Cave deck, steel cables run between wood posts under a wide, flat top rail. Cable keeps the Hill Country view open where a picket rail would block it.

The stair to the upper level carries the same cable railing, and its side is clad in horizontal boards.

The East Austin deck has no railing. It sits at the level of the porch floor and meets the pool coping.

How Should a Deck Meet the Rest of the Yard?

The edges are where the design shows.

In Bee Cave the deck edge meets steel. Beside the deck, steel walls hold level terraces of dark gravel that step with the slope, and a step is set into the first wall. The deck and the ground around it read as one design. See our steel retaining walls.

In East Austin the deck is the floor of a connected outdoor room. It links the screened porch, the pool, and the outdoor kitchen. The kitchen stands at the far end of the deck, in front of a custom Corten steel screen. The screen is perforated in a dot pattern and backlit with LED strip, so it is art by day and a glowing panel after dark. A horizontal cedar privacy fence with string lights closes the room.

Questions We Hear

What should I look for in a deck builder in Austin?

Five answers before any board is ordered: the height of the finished deck, the frame, the board for the sun, the railing, and what happens where the deck ends. Ask to see a finished deck for each one.

Do I need a permit to build a deck in Austin?

Austin Development Services lists a deck as exempt from a building permit only when it is 200 square feet or less, no more than 30 inches above grade at any point, not attached to the house, not a way out of the house, and outside a flood hazard area. A deck that misses any one of those conditions needs a permit.

What do you build decks from?

The East Austin deck is teak. We also build in IPE hardwood, Western red cedar, thermally modified wood, and composite, on frames of mild steel.

Is cable railing a good choice for a deck with a view?

Yes. On our Bee Cave deck, steel cables run between wood posts under a flat top rail, and the Hill Country view stays open.

Can a deck be built level with a porch and a pool?

Yes. Our East Austin teak deck runs flush from a glass-walled screened porch to an outdoor kitchen, with no grade change and no transition strip.
