Firecracker Fern in Austin, TX: Red Flowers That Spill Over an Edge

November 10, 2026

Firecracker fern (Russelia equisetiformis) is not a fern but a flowering perennial with thread-like green stems and red tubular flowers. The City of Austin’s Grow Green plant guide lists it at 3 feet tall and wide, for sun or part shade, with low water needs, and notes that it attracts hummingbirds. In these photos it spills over the steel edge of a front-yard terrace in South Austin.

What Is Firecracker Fern?

Grow Green lists it as a perennial and notes that it is not a true fern. It looks the part, though: a fountain of thin green stems with very little leaf.

The flowers are small red tubes, carried along the stems. Grow Green lists red flowers, and a white form as well, with interest from spring to winter. Central Texas Gardener, the Austin PBS gardening show, records the bloom from late spring through summer and says hummingbirds love it.

On size, the two sources agree on the width. Grow Green lists 3 feet tall and wide. Central Texas Gardener puts it at about 3 feet wide and notes that the drooping habit makes it look shorter than its listed height.

What Does It Need?

  • Light: sun or part shade
  • Water: low. Central Texas Gardener calls it drought-tough.
  • Soil: Grow Green lists moderately rich, well-drained soil
  • Pruning: Grow Green advises pruning spent flower stalks

Note the soil line. Firecracker fern asks for a richer soil than the agaves and yuccas beside it on this terrace, so give it its own pocket of amended soil, with the same fast drainage.

Part shade is useful too. It gives you a red-flowering spill plant for a bed that gets sun for only part of the day.

What Happens to It in Winter?

In a mild winter it stays green, and in a harsh one it freezes back and returns. Grow Green lists firecracker fern as evergreen. Central Texas Gardener notes that it can freeze to the ground in a harsh winter and that it returns from the roots.

Austin gets both kinds of winter. The USDA’s 2023 hardiness map puts most of the Austin area in zone 9a, where the coldest night of an average winter falls between 20 and 25°F. Camp Mabry also recorded 15°F in December 2022 and single digits in February 2021.

The care is simple either way. Central Texas Gardener recommends shearing it to about six inches in late winter, and new stems follow in spring.

Design for the cut-back weeks. On a steel edge, the steel still gives a finished line while the plant regrows.

Where Does Firecracker Fern Fit in a Design?

Put it at the top of an edge. Central Texas Gardener notes that its cascading habit suits raised beds, and that is how it is used on this terrace: planted behind the steel, with the stems trailing over the face.

  • On a steel wall: a spill plant softens the top line of a steel retaining wall. On sloped lots like those in Westlake Hills, every tier has an edge for one.
  • In a planter: it trails over the lip of a custom steel planter. See our steel planter ideas.
  • As contrast: soft stems beside stiff forms. On this terrace it grows under the crowns of Yucca rostrata and near giant hesperaloe, and the red flowers stand out against black steel.

Watch where the stems land. A plant 3 feet wide at the top of a low wall reaches whatever is below it, so keep a walk or a step clear of the spill, or set the plant back from that stretch of edge.
