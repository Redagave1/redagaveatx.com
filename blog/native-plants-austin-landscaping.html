Native Plants for Austin Landscaping

Why Go Native

Grow With Austin's Ecology

Native plants are the foundation of a thriving Austin landscape. They've evolved to thrive in our hot, dry summers and alkaline caliche soil—no extra water, no special fertilizers. Native species anchor pollinators, reduce your water bill, and handle our local climate without stress. Whether you're building from scratch or refreshing an existing garden, native plants eliminate guesswork.

Red Agave designs Austin landscapes around native plants because they work. We source from local growers, install with proper spacing for mature size, and handle soil prep for optimal establishment. The result: a garden that looks better year after year with less maintenance.

FAQs

Native Plants Answered

Do native plants need fertilizer?
No. Austin's native plants evolved to thrive in our rocky, alkaline soil. Over-fertilizing can weaken them and promote weak, leggy growth. Mulch with 2–3 inches of wood chips at planting; that's all the nutrition they need.
What's the best time to plant natives in Austin?
Fall (September–November) is ideal—cooler temps, natural rainfall, and time for root establishment before heat. Spring is second-best (March–April). Avoid summer planting unless you commit to daily watering.
How far apart should I space native plants?
Space trees 30–60 feet apart for mature canopy width. Shrubs 8–12 feet. Use mature width (not current container size) to calculate spacing. Tight planting looks good now but creates crowding issues later.
Do I need to amend Austin's caliche soil for natives?
Not aggressively. We typically break through or grind caliche in planting holes and amend with native topsoil to improve drainage. Most Texas-native plants tolerate rocky soil—that's the point. Avoid heavy compost unless planting shade-tolerant species that prefer moisture.
Can I use native plants with hardscape like concrete and steel?
Absolutely. Natives pair beautifully with red Agave's steel edging, stamped concrete, and fire pits. The contrast between refined hardscape and naturalistic planting is a signature look in modern Austin design.
Will native plants attract pollinators and wildlife?
Yes. Black-eyed Susan, autumn sage, lantana, and esperanza attract hummingbirds and bees. Desert marigold and Mealy Blue Sage feed native pollinators. Agarito, live oak, and sycamore provide cover and food for songbirds. A native plant garden becomes an extension of Austin's natural systems.
