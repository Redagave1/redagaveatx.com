1985

A Neighborhood Built Around a Golf Course

Great Hills grew up in the 1970s and 1980s around what is now Great Hills Country Club. The clubhouse went up in the late 1970s, and in 1985 the members bought the golf club and made it a private club. Yards from that era have had forty years to settle, and their trees forty years to grow.

Bull Creek

Runoff Heads for Salamander Streams

Great Hills Park lies in the Bull Creek watershed. The Jollyville Plateau salamander lives primarily in the springs and streams of northwest Austin and southern Williamson County, and it was listed as threatened under the Endangered Species Act in 2013. The City of Austin reports that its populations have declined in urbanized watersheds.

1 day

One Sprinkler Day, Two Drip Days

Great Hills is inside Austin city limits and follows Austin Water’s rules. Under the Conservation Stage in effect since September 2, 2025, automatic sprinklers run one day a week by address, midnight to 10 a.m. or 7 p.m. to midnight. Drip irrigation and hose-end sprinklers get two days.

What That Means for the Design

Plan the Water,
Then Build the Hill

On a hillside lot, rain comes in from the lot above and leaves toward the lot below, and the water that leaves a Great Hills yard heads downhill toward creeks like the ones in Great Hills Park. So the plan starts with drainage: where water enters, where it has to pass the house, and where it can slow down and soak in. Swales and dry creek beds carry it on the surface; French drains, catch basins, and channel drains carry it where it cannot stay on top. We use both rigid PVC and corrugated pipe and pick by the run. Our drainage service covers each system.

Then the slope becomes ground you can use. Steel walls cut it into level terraces of gravel or planting, each with a perforated drain pipe in clean gravel at its base, and a deck takes over where the grade drops too fast to fill. See our steel retaining walls and custom decks.

Great Hills is also inside Austin’s tree ordinance: trees 19 inches in diameter or larger, measured 4.5 feet up, are protected, and listed species 24 inches and up are heritage trees. Every landscape design we draw here is laid out around the trunks first, and the beds go on drip so they get both of Austin’s drip days. Warm, low light finishes the slope at night; our landscape lighting service explains the system.

Planting

Shade, Rock,
and Slope

Under oaks and junipers, plant for dry shade: inland sea oats, Turk’s cap, American beautyberry, and cedar sage. On the sunny parts of a slope, bunch grasses and agaves hold the soil between terraces and live on little water. Our native plants guide has the full list.

Natives also need little or no fertilizer, which matters when the runoff heads for spring-fed creeks. Every bed goes on drip, which Austin allows two days a week. Our water conservation guide covers the rest.

Featured Plant · Great Hills

Cedar Sage

Salvia roemeriana

Cedar sage grows wild in the shade of cedar brakes and oak woods, on rocky limestone: the dry shade under oaks and junipers where grass gives up. It blooms red from spring into late summer, rates high for deer resistance, and stays at 1 to 2 feet, small enough to fill the beds between terraces. Give it dappled shade, not full sun.

Questions From Great Hills

Slopes, Water, and Trees

Do you build landscapes in Great Hills and Jollyville?
Yes. Red Agave designs and builds luxury landscapes across Great Hills, Jollyville, and the rest of northwest Austin: drainage, steel terraces and retaining walls, decks, patios, lighting, and planting. Every plan is owner-designed and built by our own crew, and the consultation and CAD plan are free.
How often can I water in Great Hills?
Great Hills follows Austin Water’s rules. Under the Conservation Stage, in effect since September 2, 2025, automatic sprinklers run one day a week: Thursday for even addresses, Wednesday for odd, midnight to 10 a.m. or 7 p.m. to midnight. Drip and hose-end sprinklers get two days, and hand watering is allowed any time.
How do you fix drainage on a sloped lot?
We start with where the water comes from and where it has to go. Swales and dry creek beds carry it across the surface; French drains, catch basins, and channel drains carry it in pipe past the house to a proper outlet. We use both rigid PVC and corrugated pipe, and behind every steel retaining wall a perforated drain pipe sits in clean gravel at the base.
Is my tree protected in Great Hills?
Great Hills is inside Austin city limits, so Austin’s tree ordinance applies. Trees 19 inches in diameter or larger, measured 4.5 feet above the ground, are protected, and listed species 24 inches and up are heritage trees. Removing a protected tree takes a city permit, so we lay out every plan around the trunks first.
Why does runoff matter in northwest Austin?
Great Hills Park lies in the Bull Creek watershed, and the Jollyville Plateau salamander, listed as threatened in 2013, lives primarily in the springs and streams of northwest Austin and southern Williamson County. Slowing water on the lot, letting it soak in, and planting natives that need little fertilizer sends cleaner water downhill.
