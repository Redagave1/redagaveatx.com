Water, Lawn, and Fire:
Three Leander Rules

Red Agave designs and builds landscapes for Leander around the city’s water, lawn, and fire rules: gas fire pits set in gravel courts edged in 1/4-inch steel, concrete patios, and native beds that live on one watering day a week. Owner-designed and built by our own crew, with a free consultation and the CAD plan included. Call or text (512) 695-7566.
1 day

One Watering Day, Drip Included

Lake Travis is Leander’s source of water. As of November 2026 the city is in Phase 2 of its water conservation plan: automatic systems, hose-end sprinklers, soaker hoses, and drip irrigation run one day a week by address, from midnight to 7 a.m. or 7 p.m. to midnight. Hand-held watering is allowed at any time.

1/2

Lawn Capped at Half the Lot

Leander’s zoning ordinance limits turfgrass on single-family lots to one half of the lot after impervious cover is subtracted, a limit in place since 2022. Any turfgrass installed has to be a variety with summer dormancy.

8 ft

Fire Sits 8 Feet Clear

Leander prohibits outdoor burning inside the city limits, with an exception for cooking or warming in a device built for that purpose. The Fire Department’s burn rules define a recreational fire as one in a non-combustible fire pit at least 8 feet from any structure or combustibles.

Sources, read November 20, 2026: City of Leander Phase 2 water conservation, Composite Zoning Ordinance (Article VI, Section 1), city FAQ, and Fire Department outdoor burn rules.

What That Means for the Design

Shrink the Lawn,
Build the Court

In Leander even drip runs one day a week, and the lawn is capped. So the part of the yard you use every week should be the part that needs no water: a court of gravel held by 1/4-inch steel edging, with concrete pads, boulders, and a fire pit at the center. Leander’s ordinance names crushed granite and decorative groundcover as alternatives to turf, and it holds non-plant material to half of the lot, so the court gets a size on the plan and the rest is planted. See our steel edging service.

The fire is gas. Leander’s ordinance makes it unlawful to burn grass, timber, and leaves inside the city limits, and a gas fire pit lights instantly with no smoke and no ash. We build them as floating welded steel, steel pans set between boulders, and fire pits carved into boulders. On the plan the fire pit sits at least 8 feet from the house, the fence, and anything else that burns, and a licensed plumber makes the gas line connection. See our custom fire pits and the gas and wood comparison.

Plant on Leander’s calendar. The city can grant 21-day watering variances for new landscaping, but none from May 10 to September 1, so new beds go in outside that window, and October and November are the best months for natives. What lawn stays goes where it gets used: the ordinance recommends buffalo grass, zoysia, or non-seeding Bermuda for sunny areas and does not count St. Augustine as drought tolerant. See the best time to landscape and our turf installation service.

Planting

Plants for One
Watering Day

The City of Leander’s 2015 wildfire protection plan puts the Hill Country on the west side of the city and the Blackland Prairie on the east, and calls the area’s soils thin and easily disturbed. On fast-draining ground the agaves and yuccas lead: Whale’s Tongue, Artichoke, and softleaf yucca. On clay, Texas sage, autumn sage, flame acanthus, and Gulf muhly take heavy alkaline soil.

Native plants need supplemental water only during their first summer, and every bed goes on drip, set for the one day your address is allowed. Our native plants guide and summer survivors list have the full palette.

Featured Plant · Leander

Flame Acanthus

Anisacanthus quadrifidus var. wrightii

Flame acanthus fits a city that waters once a week. The Wildflower Center rates its water use low and its drought tolerance high, and notes that rain triggers a flush of blooms. It flowers red-orange from June to October and grows in sand, loam, clay, caliche, and limestone, so it works on both sides of Leander. Its flowers bring hummingbirds to the beds around a fire court.

Questions From Leander

Water, Lawn, and Fire

Do you build landscapes in Leander?
Yes. Leander is in our service area. Red Agave Landscape designs and builds gas fire pits, gravel courts edged in 1/4-inch steel, patios, lighting, and native planting. Every plan is owner-designed and built by our own crew, and the consultation is free with the CAD plan included.
How often can I water in Leander?
As of November 2026 Leander is in Phase 2 of its water conservation plan. Automatic systems, hose-end sprinklers, soaker hoses, and drip irrigation run one day a week, from midnight to 7 a.m. or 7 p.m. to midnight. The day follows the last digit of your address: 2 or 4 Monday, 1 or 5 Tuesday, 6 Wednesday, 0 Thursday, 9 Friday, 8 Saturday, 3 or 7 Sunday. Hand-held watering is allowed at any time.
How much lawn can a Leander yard have?
Leander’s zoning ordinance limits turfgrass on single-family lots to one half of the lot after impervious cover is subtracted. The limit was two-thirds of a residential lot from 2014 and has been one half since 2022. The version that applies to a subdivision is set by the date of its developer’s first application.
Can I have a fire pit in Leander?
Yes, within the city’s rules. Leander prohibits outdoor burning inside the city limits, and its Fire Department’s burn rules allow burning for cooking or warming in a device built for that purpose. They define a recreational fire as one in a non-combustible fire pit at least 8 feet from any structure or combustibles, and they prohibit all burning during burn bans. The Leander Fire Marshal’s Office answers questions about burning inside the city.
Can my HOA require a lawn in Leander?
No. Leander’s zoning ordinance says a property owners’ association cannot require a homeowner to install a minimum percentage of turf or a specific variety of turf grass, and cannot prohibit drip irrigation or rain barrels.
