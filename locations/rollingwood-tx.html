The Ground You're Working With

What Rollingwood Asks
Before You Build

Red Agave designs and builds outdoor living for Rollingwood yards around the trees that define them: decks that sit on a few footings instead of a slab, warm lighting in the canopy, and native planting that gets by on Austin’s watering limits. Owner-designed, built by our own crew. Rollingwood requires a city permit for any tree trimming or removal, so every plan starts with the canopy.
Permit

Every Tree Cut Needs a Permit

Rollingwood requires a city permit for any tree trimming or removal inside city limits. Oaks cannot be trimmed from February 1 through June 30, the months when oak wilt spreads most easily, and violations carry a fine. The city adopted an updated Residential Landscaping and Tree Canopy Management Ordinance in April 2025.

1963

Austin Water, Austin Rules

Rollingwood runs its own water utility but buys its water from Austin under an agreement that dates to 1963, and its watering schedule follows Austin’s stages. Under Conservation Stage rules, automatic irrigation runs one day a week by address, midnight to 5 a.m. or 7 p.m. to midnight.

1955

A Small City With Its Own Garden

Rollingwood incorporated as a village in 1955 and became a city in 1963. Its City Hall Waterwise Garden, watered from a rainwater cistern, names the conditions that decide what grows here: soil, sunlight, and deer resistance.

What That Means for the Design

Float the Deck,
Keep the Canopy

On a Rollingwood lot the trees come first, because the city decides what happens to them. A poured slab covers the ground a tree feeds through; a deck on a few footings leaves most of it open to air and water. That is why the outdoor room here is usually a deck: IPE, Western Red Cedar, or composite on a mild steel frame, set around the trunks. Our custom decks service covers the materials and framing.

Around the deck, native beds fill the sunny gaps, and lighting follows the trees: warm 24V FCOB along the deck edge and COB uplights into the canopy. Our landscape lighting service explains the system, and the Tarrytown page shows the same oak-canopy approach.

Any trimming the plan needs goes through the city’s permit first, and oak pruning waits until after June 30. It all starts with a landscape design drawn around the trees you have.

Planting

Shade, Sun Gaps,
and Deer

Under the oaks, the shade natives that work in Tarrytown work here: inland sea oats, Turk’s cap, cedar sage, and American beautyberry. In the sunny gaps between canopies, agaves and Texas sage take the heat. Our cold-hardy agave guide covers the agaves.

The city’s own garden names deer resistance as a local condition, so lean on the plants deer leave alone, like Texas sage and agaves. Our native plants guide has the full list.

Featured Plant · Rollingwood

Mexican Buckeye

Ungnadia speciosa

A small native tree that blooms pink in spring and turns gold in fall, sized to sit under Rollingwood’s big oaks without competing with them. The city’s own Waterwise Garden names deer resistance as a neighborhood condition, and this one rates high. Its seeds are poisonous, so keep it away from where small children play.

Questions From Rollingwood

Trees, Water, and Decks

Do I need a permit to trim or remove a tree in Rollingwood?
Yes. Rollingwood requires a city permit for any tree trimming or removal inside city limits, and oaks cannot be trimmed from February 1 through June 30. The city adopted an updated Residential Landscaping and Tree Canopy Management Ordinance in April 2025.
How often can I water in Rollingwood?
Rollingwood buys its water from Austin and follows Austin’s watering stages. Under Conservation Stage rules, automatic irrigation runs one day a week: even addresses on Thursday, odd addresses on Wednesday, midnight to 5 a.m. or 7 p.m. to midnight.
Why build a deck instead of a patio under oaks?
A deck sits on a handful of footings, so most of the ground under it stays open to air and water. A poured slab covers all of it. Under protected trees that difference matters, and Rollingwood’s permit rules make the trees the first thing a plan answers to.
What grows under Rollingwood’s oaks?
Shade natives like inland sea oats, Turk’s cap, cedar sage, and American beautyberry, with agaves and Texas sage in the sunny gaps. Mexican buckeye is a small native tree that fits under the big oaks and rates high for deer resistance.
Who designs and builds the project?
Red Agave does both. Every plan is owner-designed, and our own crew builds it: decks, steel, concrete, planting, and lighting.
