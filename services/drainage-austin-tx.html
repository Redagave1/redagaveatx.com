Know Your Ground

Clay East, Limestone West: Why Austin Lots Drain Differently

Texas A&M AgriLife Extension describes two very different soils in Travis County. East of I-35, Blackland Prairie clays shrink and swell with moisture, heave fence posts and patios, and absorb water very slowly. West of I-35, the Edwards Plateau has thin soil over limestone that sheds rain as runoff. The same storm causes different problems on each side, so the fix is different too.

GroundWhat Goes WrongWhat Works
Blackland clay (east)Water sits on the surface and soaks in slowly. Wet and dry cycles move slabs and foundations.Regrading away from the house, catch basins at low spots, and solid pipe to a proper outlet. Soak-in features like rain gardens have to be larger on clay.
Thin soil over limestone (west)Fast runoff and little depth to dig. Water sheets across patios and cuts through beds.Swales, dry creek beds, and channel drains that carry water across the surface to an outlet.
Solutions

Drainage Systems We Build

01
French Drains
A perforated pipe in a gravel trench wrapped in non-woven geotextile, set at a minimum 1% slope and run to a real outlet. We use both rigid PVC and corrugated pipe and pick by the run: rigid PVC holds its slope and can be cleaned out, and corrugated bends around curves.
02
Channel Drains
A grated channel set into patios, driveways, and pool decks that catches sheet flow at the surface and carries it to an outlet pipe.
03
Catch Basins
A collection box with a slotted grate at a low spot, feeding solid pipe to daylight or a pop-up emitter away from the house.
04
Regrading and Swales
Reshaping the ground so it falls away from the house, and wide, shallow swales that carry water to an outlet.
05
Downspout Routing
Downspouts tied into buried solid pipe and released well away from the foundation, often into a dry creek bed.
06
Dry Creek Beds
Rock-lined channels that carry runoff in a storm and read as a planted landscape feature the rest of the year.

Behind every steel retaining wall, a 4-inch perforated drain pipe wrapped in filter fabric sits at the base, with at least 12 inches of clean drainage gravel between the pipe and the wall, so water never builds pressure against the steel.

The Rules

Drainage Rules Every Austin Homeowner Should Know

RuleWhat It Says
Texas Water Code §11.086No one may divert or impound the natural flow of surface water in a way that damages another person’s property. The injured owner can sue for damages.
Grade at the foundation (IRC R401.3)The ground must fall at least 6 inches within the first 10 feet from the foundation. Where a lot line or wall prevents that, drains or swales carry the water away.
Roof water on expansive soil (IRC R801.3)Where soils are expansive, roof water must discharge at least 5 feet from the foundation or into an approved drainage system.
Protected trees (City of Austin)Near a tree 19 inches or larger, no cut or fill over 4 inches is allowed in the half critical root zone, and a Tree Ordinance Review Application is needed before work that may disturb the root zone.
Drainage easements (City of Austin)No permanent structures, including fences, and no planting that blocks the flow.
Floodplain (City of Austin)After the 2018 Atlas 14 study raised Austin’s 100-year, 24-hour rainfall from about 10.2 inches to almost 13 in some areas, the City applies the FEMA 500-year floodplain to development rules until remapping is finished.

The City of Austin does not manage flooding between private properties, so a drainage dispute between neighbors is a civil matter. That is why a drainage plan has to end at a proper outlet, not at the property line. You can check your lot’s flood risk at ATXFloodPro.com.

Austin Water also offers a WaterWise Rainscape rebate for features that capture and hold rainwater on site, such as rain gardens. Current terms are on Austin Water’s rebates page.

Sources: Texas Water Code §11.086, City of Austin, Trees on Residential Property, City of Austin, Drainage Easements, City of Austin, Floodplain Management, Travis County AgriLife, Austin-area soils.

Common Questions

Drainage FAQ

How much does drainage work cost in Austin?
Every drainage project is quoted after a free on-site assessment. Price depends on the length of the runs, the type of system, the outlet, and site access.
Can my neighbor drain water onto my yard?
Not by diverting it. Texas Water Code §11.086 bars anyone from diverting or impounding the natural flow of surface water in a way that damages another person’s property, and the injured owner can sue for damages. The City of Austin does not manage flooding between private properties, so it is a civil matter.
What is the best drainage solution for clay soil?
On Austin’s Blackland clay, water soaks in very slowly, so the fix is usually to collect it and move it: regrade away from the house, add catch basins at low spots, and carry the water in solid pipe to a proper outlet. Soak-in features like rain gardens have to be larger on clay.
How far should downspouts drain from the foundation?
Where soils are expansive, the International Residential Code (R801.3) calls for roof water to discharge at least 5 feet from the foundation or into an approved drainage system. We route downspouts underground to an outlet well away from the house.
Will a French drain protect my foundation?
It helps when water collects along the house. The ground also has to fall at least 6 inches within the first 10 feet of the foundation (IRC R401.3), so the fix usually pairs a drain with regrading.
Can you trench for drainage near my trees?
In Austin, near a tree 19 inches or larger, no cut or fill over 4 inches is allowed in the half critical root zone, and a Tree Ordinance Review Application is needed before work that may disturb the root zone. Drainage runs are routed around root zones.
Do you use PVC or corrugated pipe for French drains?
Both. Rigid PVC holds its slope, resists crushing, and can be cleaned through a cleanout. Corrugated pipe is cheaper and bends around curves. We pick by the run and wrap the gravel in filter fabric either way, because fine silt washing in is a common cause of clogged drains.
Do you offer free drainage assessments?
Yes. We assess drainage on site for free, and when possible we visit during or after a rain to see the problem firsthand.
