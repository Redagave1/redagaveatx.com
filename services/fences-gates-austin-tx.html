Slopes

How Does a Fence Handle a Slope?

By stepping or by racking. Few Austin lots are flat, and a fence has to meet the ground along its whole length.

  • Stepped: each section stays level, and the top of the fence drops at the posts. This suits horizontal boards, because every board stays level.
  • Racked: the panels follow the angle of the ground.

The black fence above is stepped. Each section is level, and the top of the fence drops at the posts as the lot falls away from the house.

Where a slope has to be held and not only fenced, the wall comes first. See our retaining walls page.

Cedar

Why Cedar for a Privacy Fence?

Because it lasts outdoors without help. Western red cedar holds natural oils that repel insects and resist rot with no chemical treatment. It takes stain well, and left alone it weathers to a silver gray.

We run cedar boards horizontally on our privacy fences. In East Austin a horizontal cedar privacy fence closes the pool yard, and string lights trace the fence line overhead. On another Austin yard, warm light washes a new horizontal cedar fence behind a gravel court.

For a fence that gives the same privacy from both sides, boards are overlapped with no gaps. That is board-on-board construction, and it looks the same from either yard.

Steel

Where Does Steel Go in a Fence?

In the frame, the posts, and the panels. We weld steel on site, so a gate or a panel is made to the opening and not ordered to a standard size.

  • Frames: a welded steel frame holds wood boards flat and square, as a gate or as a fixed panel.
  • Panels: we cut decorative steel panels and privacy partitions on our own CNC plasma table.
  • Trellis: steel posts and rails carry wire grid panels for vines.
  • Finish: steel is powder-coated, or it is left to weather to a rust patina.

It is the same steel work as our steel edging, our custom steel planters, and our steel retaining walls, so one material can run through the whole yard.

Walls

When Is a Wall the Better Fence?

When the fence faces the street. A masonry wall gives a front yard a solid edge, and open block keeps it from feeling shut.

A wood gate stands in the middle, steel planters sit at the foot of the wall, and square concrete pads in gravel lead to the gate.

Austin Rules

Does a Fence Need a Permit in Austin?

Most do not. The rules that matter are height, floodplain, pools, and the HOA.

QuestionAnswer
Building permitThe City of Austin lists fences under 7 feet tall, outside a flood hazard area, as work that needs no building permit.
PoolsA pool needs a barrier at least 48 inches tall, with a self-closing, self-latching gate.
HOATexas law prevents an HOA from banning a perimeter fence. It can regulate materials, color, height, and style.
DiggingUtility lines are marked through 811 before any post hole is dug.

We handle HOA applications, property line verification, 811 calls, and code compliance as part of every fence project. Our fence permit guide covers height by location on the lot, corner lots, and easements.

How We Build

How Is a Fence Built?

01
Consultation and Plan

Free and on site. We walk the property with you, measure the fence line, and look at the soil and the slope. The fence goes on the CAD plan, which is included.

02
Lines and Approvals

We verify the property line, file the HOA application, and make the 811 call before any digging.

03
Posts

Posts go in first. Much of Austin sits on limestone, and rock in the ground changes how the post holes are made.

04
Panels and Gates

Boards go on post to post. Steel frames for gates and panels are welded to the opening.

05
Finish

Cedar is stained or left to weather. Steel is powder-coated or left to weather. Lighting and planting are fitted to the finished fence.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who builds custom fences and gates in Austin, TX?
Red Agave Landscape designs and builds custom fences and gates in Austin in cedar and mild steel: privacy fences, property dividers, decorative metal panels, and gates in welded steel frames. Every fence is built by our own crew and starts with a free consultation, with the CAD plan included.
What fence materials do you build with?
Cedar and mild steel. We use western red cedar for privacy fencing and powder-coated steel for decorative and front yard fencing. We also build walls of stucco and breeze block where a wall is the better fence.
Do you build horizontal fences?
Yes. In East Austin we built a horizontal cedar privacy fence around a pool yard. On a sloped lot we built a black horizontal fence that steps down the hill in level sections.
Do you build custom gates?
Yes. We weld the gate frame in steel and fill it with wood. One of our gates holds weathered boards laid on the diagonal in a welded steel frame. Another is a gate of vertical wood boards set in a breeze block wall.
Can a fence follow a sloped yard?
Yes. A fence on a slope is built with stepped or racked panels. A stepped fence keeps each section level and drops at the posts.
Do I need a permit for a fence in Austin?
Most residential fences in Austin do not need a building permit. The city lists fences under 7 feet tall, outside a flood hazard area, as exempt work. Our fence permit guide covers the height rules, corner lots, and the safe fencing code.
Can my HOA stop me from building a fence?
No. Texas law prevents an HOA from banning a perimeter fence. An HOA can still regulate the materials, the color, the height, and the style, and many require approval before construction.
Does a pool need a fence in Austin?
Yes. The City of Austin requires a barrier around every residential pool that is at least 48 inches tall, with a self-closing, self-latching gate. We include the fence permitting, and we design the barrier as part of the landscape.
Can a fence carry vines or lighting?
Yes. In East Austin we built a steel trellis with wire grid panels that carries jasmine around a seating court. On another yard, warm light washes a new horizontal cedar fence at dusk.
What does a fence cost in Austin?
Every fence is priced after a free on-site consultation. The length, the material, the height, the slope, the rock in the ground, and the number of gates drive the price. Our fence cost guide explains each one.
