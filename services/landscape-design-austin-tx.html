Owner-Designed

How Does Red Agave Design a Landscape?

At your property, with the build in mind. A landscape design is only as good as what gets built from it. We draw every plan knowing how our crew will build it: how the steel gets cut and welded, how deep the base goes, and where the water will run.

Design starts at your property, not in an office. We walk the yard with you, study the grade, trace the shade lines, and photograph the sightlines from inside the house. Then we look at what the site demands: soil, drainage, existing trees, views, and privacy.

When the plan is approved, the same team builds it. See how that works on our design and build page.

Designed for Austin

What Do Austin Sites Change in a Design?

The soil, the trees, the water rules, and the heat. A design that works in Dallas or Phoenix can fail here. West Austin and the Hill Country sit on thin soil over limestone and caliche. East of the Balcones Escarpment, the ground turns to Blackland Prairie clay that swells when wet and shrinks when dry. Every plan starts from those facts.

Site ConditionWhat It Changes in the Design
Expansive clayA deeper compacted base under every slab, grading that moves water away from the house, and planting beds built up with amended soil
Thin soil over limestone or calicheTest holes before the planting plan, raised steel beds where roots need depth, and plants that take alkaline soil
Protected treesThe layout works around them. In Austin a tree 19 inches or more in diameter, measured 4.5 feet above the ground, is protected, and removing it takes a city permit (City Arborist).
Watering limitsDrought-tolerant plants on drip irrigation, with lawn only where it gets used. Under Austin Water's Conservation Stage rules, automatic irrigation systems run one day a week, and hose-end sprinklers and drip irrigation run two (Austin Water).
DeerA deer-resistant planting palette in west Austin and the Hill Country
Summer heatPlants that hold up in a 100°F summer, and surfaces like travertine that stay cooler underfoot

Each city adds its own rules. See what changes in Westlake Hills, Lakeway, Cedar Park, and Dripping Springs.

Who Draws It

Landscape Designer, Design-Build Firm, or Landscape Architect?

They are three different things, and Texas law draws the line. Red Agave is a landscape design and build firm. We are not landscape architects.

TitleWhat It Means in Texas
Landscape architectA person registered with the state. Only a registered person uses this title.
Landscape designerDraws landscape plans. The law lets landscape designers draw plans without that registration.
Landscape contractorBuilds the landscape. The law lets landscape contractors draw plans too.
Design-build firmOne company draws the plan and builds it. Red Agave works this way: the owner draws, and our own crew builds.

The law is Texas Occupations Code, Chapter 1052. Section 1052.003 also lets anyone draw plans for a single-family residence without that registration.

What matters more than the title is whether the person drawing knows how the yard gets built. Nothing on our plans is a guess about construction, because the people who draw them build them. For help comparing companies, read how to choose a landscaper.

Phases + Approvals

Can a Design Be Built in Phases?

Yes. We draw the whole property at once, even if you build it in phases. Infrastructure like drainage, electrical, and gas is planned first, so later phases never tear up finished work.

  • One plan: the front yard, the back yard, and the side yards are drawn together, so each phase fits the next
  • Infrastructure first: drainage, electrical, and gas lines go in before the surfaces above them
  • HOA review: every project starts with a custom CAD design the committee can review. See our approval guides for Circle C, Steiner Ranch, and Mueller
