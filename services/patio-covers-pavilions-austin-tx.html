The Difference

Pergola, Patio Cover, Pavilion, or Screened Room?

The roof decides it. A pergola is open to the sky. The other three have a solid roof, and they differ in where they stand and what closes the sides.

StructureWhat It IsRoof
PergolaAn open frame of posts and beams, with rafters or slats on topOpen
Patio coverA solid roof attached to the house, over the patioSolid
PavilionA solid roof that stands on its own postsSolid
Screened roomA covered frame with screens and a doorSolid

One frame can be all of them, one after another. In South Austin our steel frame stood on the slab as an open pergola, then took a metal roof, then took screens. This page covers the roofed structures. For open shade, see our custom pergolas page.

South Austin

How Does a Steel Frame Become a Covered Room?

In order, with the steel first. Every later part fastens to the frame, so the steel is set and squared before anything else arrives. These are the parts of the room we built in South Austin:

  • Frame: square steel tube, welded, finished black
  • Roof: ribbed metal panels over the steel
  • Ceiling: cedar planks
  • Skylights: two, with wells lined in the same cedar
  • Fan: a black ceiling fan with a light
  • Walls: screens in black frames, with a low rail
  • Door: a screen door with a pet door built into it
  • Floor: a concrete slab

The frame runs along the back wall of the house and passes above the French doors. The posts are slim, so the walls are almost all screen.

The full sequence is in our post on the custom pergola build in South Austin.

Overhead

What Roof and Ceiling Do We Build?

A solid roof on top and a finished ceiling under it. The roof keeps the weather out, and the ceiling is the part you look at.

  • Roof: ribbed metal panels, standing seam metal, or cedar shingle
  • Ceiling: cedar planks or tongue-and-groove wood boards
  • Skylights: an opening for each one is welded into the roof framing before the roof goes on
  • Lights: recessed lights set into the ceiling boards
  • Fan: a ceiling fan, wired above the boards

Skylights answer one problem a roof creates. A solid roof over the back doors shades them, and the skylights bring daylight back in. In South Austin the cedar ceiling is carried up into each opening, so each skylight sits at the top of a cedar well.

The wiring is hidden. We run electrical through the posts and beams, so no conduit shows, and we coordinate with a licensed electrician for the panel connection.

West Lake Hills

What Goes Under a Roof Built for a Kitchen?

Everything the cook needs overhead. In West Lake Hills the outdoor kitchen stands under its own flat roof on black posts, with a tongue-and-groove wood ceiling and a back wall clad in horizontal wood boards.

Building the roof and the kitchen together let us put each part where it belongs:

  • Four recessed lights over the counter
  • A ceiling fan centered over the cook
  • A TV on the back wall, above the counter
  • A light strip under the counter edge that washes the slab at night

The kitchen itself has a stainless built-in grill, a sink, and two under-counter refrigerators in a dark brick base with a stone countertop. A kitchen with its own roof can sit wherever the yard works best. See our outdoor kitchens page and our post on outdoor kitchens under a roof.

Austin Weather

Why Put a Solid Roof Over a Patio in Austin?

For the sun and for the rain. Austin summers push past 100 degrees for weeks at a time, and the rain tends to arrive all at once.

  • Sun: a solid roof gives full shade, where a pergola gives partial shade.
  • Rain: the City of Austin describes the region as Flash Flood Alley, and normal rainfall at Camp Mabry is 36.25 inches a year. A roof keeps it off the counters, the appliances, and the furniture.
  • Air: a roof gives you a ceiling, and a ceiling carries a fan to keep the air moving on a still summer evening.
  • Light: the same ceiling holds the lights, so the room works after dark.

A roof turns a patio into a room. See how the rest of the room comes together on our outdoor living page, and compare the options in our shade structure ideas.

Permits

Does a Patio Cover Need a Permit in Austin?

Attachment and size decide it. A roofed structure attached to a house is permitted differently from an open pergola in the yard.

StructureCity of Austin
Solid roof attached to the houseBuilding permit, with structural drawings
One-story detached structure, 200 square feet or less and 15 feet tall or less, with no plumbing, outside a flood hazard areaOn the city's list of work exempt from a building permit
Any structure with a solid roofCounts toward the impervious cover limit of the lot
Open pergola with no solid roofDoes not count as impervious cover

Exempt work still has to meet the building code and the city code. Cedar Park and the other cities around Austin have their own rules, and many HOA communities review exterior changes from a drawn plan.

We handle the permit and the plans. Our patio cover permit guide covers setbacks, impervious cover, and what the city asks for.

The Build

How Is a Patio Cover Built?

From the plan up. These are the steps, in the order we built the South Austin room.

01
Consultation and Plan

Free and on site. We walk the yard with you, and the owner draws the structure on a CAD plan, which is included. See our landscape design page.

02
Permit

We prepare the plans and handle the permit where the structure needs one.

03
Slab and Frame

The frame goes up on the slab first, and alone. Posts stand at the corners and along the outer edge, and the roof framing sits on top.

04
Roof and Skylights

The skylight openings are already in the framing. The roof panels go on over the steel.

05
Ceiling and Wiring

Wiring for the lights and the fan runs above the boards, and then the ceiling closes.

06
Screens and Finish

Screen panels go in between the posts, the door is hung, and the fan goes up.

The patio under the roof is part of the same job. We coordinate the patio with the structure above it, so the footings, the columns, and the drainage work together. See our patios and custom decks pages.
