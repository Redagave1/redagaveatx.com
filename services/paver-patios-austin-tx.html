The Work

What Paver Patios Do We Build?

A paver patio is a floor made of separate pieces. The pieces sit on a compacted base, and the joints between them let the floor move with the ground. We build with four kinds of paver.

Austin Clay

Why Pavers on Austin Clay?

Because the ground here moves. Austin clay swells when it is wet and shrinks when it is dry, and whatever is built on it has to answer that.

  • The joints take the movement: each paver is free to shift a little, so the floor flexes where a single slab is rigid.
  • A low spot is repaired, not replaced: a paver that settles is lifted, the base under it is leveled, and the paver is set back.
  • One paver can be swapped: a damaged unit comes out, and a new one goes in.
  • Pipes stay reachable: pavers are pulled to reach a line under the patio and set back when the work is done.
  • Water gets through: the joints let rain drain into the base.

Below the Surface

How Is a Paver Patio Built?

From the bottom up. The base is the part you never see, and it decides whether the patio stays flat.

01
Excavate and Compact

We dig to the depth the pavers and their base need, and compact the soil at the bottom.

02
Fabric

Woven geotextile fabric goes over the clay, so the clay stays out of the base above it.

03
Base

6 to 8 inches of crushed limestone, compacted, and graded so water moves away from the house.

04
Setting Bed

One inch of coarse sand or screenings, screeded flat. This is the final leveling layer.

05
Set the Pavers

Pavers go down on the bed with consistent joints, in the pattern on the plan.

06
Edge

Steel edging goes around the perimeter. It holds the field square and keeps the pavers from creeping.

07
Joints

Polymeric sand is swept into tight joints. Wide joints are filled with gravel, river rock, or turf. A plate compactor with a rubber pad seats the pavers.

The patio is drawn on the CAD plan first, with every run dimensioned. See our landscape design page.

Pattern

Which Paver Patterns Do We Lay?

The pattern that suits the house and the shape of the yard. These are five we have built.

  • Grid with bands: pale square pavers, with a darker paver between them as a band and around them as a border.
  • Herringbone: small charcoal pavers locked at right angles. The pattern suits a walk or a drive.
  • Long pads in a row: two-by-four-foot limestone pavers laid end to end down a side yard.
  • Diagonal cuts: large concrete pavers cut on the diagonal, so the joints run at an angle to the house.
  • Cut to a curve: the outer pavers are cut to follow a curved steel edge.
The Edge

What Holds the Edge of a Paver Patio?

Steel. Pavers need a restraint at the perimeter, or the outer rows creep outward and the joints open. We build paver patios with steel edging at every perimeter.

Steel also does more than hold. On the grid patio, the pavers sit on a raised steel edge, and three steel-edged beds of black gravel step down from the corner to the lawn. Along the porch, a strip of black beach pebbles fills the gap between the porch slab and the pavers.

The edge can be lit. We set 24V lighting in steel edges and in paver joints, so the outline of the patio shows after dark. See our steel edging and landscape lighting pages.

Heat

Which Pavers Stay Cool in the Austin Sun?

The light ones. Color and material decide how hot a patio gets underfoot.

  • Travertine: stays cooler than concrete in direct sun, which is why it is used around pools.
  • Light pavers: limestone, cream, and light gray reflect more sunlight and stay cooler.
  • Charcoal pavers: the hottest in full sun. They suit a shaded patio, a walk, or a drive.

Shade does the rest. A roof or a pergola over the patio is drawn on the same plan, so the posts land where the pavers allow. See our pergolas page and our patio covers and pavilions page. Our guide to travertine, flagstone, and pavers compares the three.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who builds paver patios in Austin, TX?
Red Agave Landscape designs and builds paver patios in Austin in manufactured concrete pavers, large-format pavers, limestone and travertine pavers, and concrete pavers poured in place. Every patio sits on 6 to 8 inches of compacted crushed limestone with steel edging at the perimeter, and our own crew builds it.
What goes under a paver patio?
A compacted base. We excavate, compact the subgrade, lay geotextile fabric over the clay, and build 6 to 8 inches of compacted crushed limestone. The pavers are set on a 1-inch bed of coarse sand or screenings.
Do pavers crack on Austin clay?
A paver patio moves with the ground. The joints between the pavers take up the movement of the clay, where a single slab is rigid. A paver that settles is lifted, the base under it is leveled, and the paver is set back.
What size pavers do you install?
Large-format pavers are 24 by 24 inches and 24 by 48 inches. In South Austin we set limestone pavers that are two feet by four feet. Concrete pavers poured in place are made to the size on the plan.
What goes in the joints between pavers?
Polymeric sand for a tight joint. For a wide joint we use gravel, river rock, or artificial turf. On one patio the joint is a band of darker pavers.
Can you put turf between pavers?
Yes. In Cedar Creek we poured large concrete pavers around a pool and ran a strip of artificial turf through every joint.
Which pavers stay coolest in the Austin sun?
Travertine. It stays cooler than concrete in direct sun. Light colors reflect more sunlight than dark ones, and charcoal pavers get the hottest.
Are pavers better than poured concrete?
Each suits a different job. Pavers flex with the ground, and a single paver can be lifted and replaced. Poured concrete gives one clean, seamless surface. We build both.
Do I need a permit for a paver patio in Austin?
Most ground-level patios do not need a building permit. A patio with a roofed structure, an attachment to the foundation of the house, or electrical or plumbing work is different, and we handle those permits.
What does a paver patio cost in Austin?
Every patio is priced after a free on-site consultation. The size, the paver, the base work, the edging, and the access to the yard drive the price. Our patio cost guide explains each one.
