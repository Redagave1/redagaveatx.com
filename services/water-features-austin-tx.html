Who Builds Custom Water Features in Austin, TX?

Red Agave Landscape designs and builds custom water features in Austin, TX in concrete, plaster, steel, and stone. We build raised pools with a plaster interior, boulders drilled to run water, open steel channels that carry water to a fall, and fire pits set beside the water. In Southeast Austin we built a round water feature the way a swimming pool is built, with a concrete shell, a plaster interior, and a sand pool filter. We have built in Austin since 2009. Built Like a Pool

How Is a Raised Pool Water Feature Built?

The same way a swimming pool is built, at a much smaller size. Our round water feature in Southeast Austin has the three parts of a pool:

  • Concrete shell: a round concrete wall, formed and poured in place
  • Plaster: the finished surface inside the shell
  • Sand pool filter: the water circulates through it, the way the water in a swimming pool does

Each part has a job. The shell holds a true circle and carries the cap on its rim. The plaster gives the inside its finished surface. The filter keeps the water clear.

The inside is finished black, and that sets the look of the whole space. Black water works like a dark mirror. It reflects the fire in front of it and the evening sky above it.

The full build, from the excavation to the finished yard, is in our post on the water feature and fire pits in Southeast Austin. For full-size projects, see our pool construction and container plunge pool pages.

The Source

Where Does the Water Come From?

From something worth looking at. A water feature has a source and a fall, and both are part of the design.

In Southeast Austin the source is a boulder with a hole drilled in it. Water comes out of the boulder and into an open steel channel, and the channel carries it to the pool, where it falls to the surface.

  • The boulder: set early, at the same stage as the excavation, because it is part of the structure of the design
  • The channel: open steel, so you watch the water travel before it drops
  • The fall: it moves the black surface, and it is the sound of the space

The channel is welded steel, like our steel edging and our custom steel planters. We cut and weld steel on site.

Stone

What Is a Drilled Boulder Fountain?

A natural boulder with a hole drilled through it and a water line run to it, so water comes out of the stone. There is no spout, no basin on a pedestal, and no hardware in view.

In Pearson Ranch, in Northwest Austin, we built two. The boulders stand on either side of a planting bed beside the patio. They are drilled and plumbed to run water from the top, and mist rises between them.

  • Sound without a pond: the water runs over stone and back into the bed
  • Planting right against it: river fern, creeping Jenny, and horsetail reed grow around the boulders
  • Stone below: river rock fills the bed between the boulders
  • Fire in the same bed: a floating steel fire pit sits beside the fountains

We work boulders more than one way. We drill them for water, cut slots in them for steel edging, and carve fire pits into them. See how we cut steel into boulders, and the Pearson Ranch project.

Fire + Water

Why Put Fire Beside Water?

Because each one makes the other look better. Water gives the fire a surface to reflect on, and fire gives the water light after dark.

In Southeast Austin a floating steel fire pit curves along the front of the pool. It sits at the foot of the pool wall, so the flames light the wall and reflect on the water. A second fire pit, a square steel pan, is suspended between two boulders behind the pool.

Red Agave is the only company in Austin that builds floating welded steel fire pits, and we carve fire pits into boulders.

See our post on custom fire pits, which shows the plan for the Southeast Austin yard next to the finished fires.

Clear Water

What Keeps the Water Clear and Moving?

A pump, a filter, and a plan for topping it off. A water feature does not use water the way irrigation does. It recirculates the same water.

PartWhat It Does
PumpMoves the water from the pool or the reservoir back to the source
FilterCleans the water as it circulates. In Southeast Austin it is a sand pool filter.
Autofill valveReplaces the water lost to evaporation
TimerRuns a fountain or a pondless feature for 12 to 16 hours a day. A pond with fish runs all day.
GFCI circuitA dedicated outdoor circuit for the pump

Moving water is also the answer to mosquitoes. Standing water breeds them, and moving water does not. Every water feature we build has a pump that keeps the surface in motion.

The upkeep is simple: clear leaves from the water, clean the pump intake, and check the water level in summer.

Austin Conditions

What Does a Water Feature Need in Austin?

A plan for heat, rock, and dry years. Each one changes how a water feature is designed here.

Austin ConditionWhat We Plan For
Summer heatAn open pond loses 1 to 2 inches of water a week in an Austin summer. An autofill valve keeps the level steady.
LimestoneParts of West Austin, Westlake Hills, and the Hill Country sit on shallow limestone. We check the depth of the rock before the design is set, and plan machine access for the dig.
Dry yearsA feature that recirculates uses far less water than the same area of lawn. A reservoir under gravel loses less to the sun than open water.
RunoffRain is carried around the water feature, not into it. Drainage is planned with the hardscape.
PowerThe circuit for the pump is run before the patio is poured.

A water feature is small next to a lawn. It suits a xeriscape yard, where gravel, boulders, and native plants do the rest of the work.

On Paper First

How Do We Design a Water Feature?

On the plan, with everything around it. A water feature is drawn with the patio, the fire, the planting, and the lighting, so the pipes and the power are settled before any concrete is poured.

The Southeast Austin yard was drawn this way. The plan put a round water feature at the corner of the patio, fed by a channel from a boulder, with two fire pits set against boulders and a dark gravel court.

Other plans put water to different uses:

  • A side yard built around water: a square pad with a narrow channel of water through its center, a pond edged with boulders above it, and a stream running to a second pond below
  • Water troughs: two small water features, each set at the edge of a planting bed beside a deck

Every design is drawn by the owner after a free on-site consultation. See them on our design plans page, and read about our landscape design.

The Build

How Is a Water Feature Built?

In the order that keeps the pipes under the concrete and the stone in the right place. These are the steps from our Southeast Austin build.

01
Consultation and Plan

We walk the yard with you, and the owner draws the plan. The consultation is free, and the CAD plan is included.

02
Dig and Set the Boulders

We excavate, set the steel edging, and place the boulders. Boulders go in early, because they are part of the structure of the design.

03
Form and Pour

The shell is formed and poured in place. When the forms come off, the wall stands as one piece of concrete.

04
Plumb and Wire

PVC lines run to the shell, with a fitting set through the wall. The line to the source and the power for the pump go in now.

05
Finish

The interior is plastered, and the outside of the wall is finished smooth. The cap is set on the rim.

06
Fill, Run, and Light

The feature is filled and the pump is started. Then the patio, the planting, and the lighting are finished around it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who builds custom water features in Austin, TX?
Red Agave Landscape designs and builds custom water features in Austin in concrete, plaster, steel, and stone. Each one is drawn by the owner after a free on-site consultation and built by our own crew.
What kinds of water features do you build?
Raised pools with a plaster interior, boulders drilled to run water, open steel channels that carry water to a fall, streams, ponds, and water walls. We also build water into swimming pools: spillover spas, bubblers, laminar jets, and sheer descent waterfalls.
Does a water feature use a lot of water?
No. A water feature recirculates its water through a pump, so the same water runs through it again and again. The only loss is evaporation, and an autofill valve replaces it.
Do water features bring mosquitoes?
Standing water breeds mosquitoes, and moving water does not. Every water feature we build has a pump that keeps the water moving.
What keeps the water clear?
Circulation and filtration. Our round water feature in Southeast Austin runs on a sand pool filter, the same way a swimming pool does.
What is a drilled boulder fountain?
A natural boulder with a hole drilled through it and a water line run to it, so water comes out of the stone. In Pearson Ranch in Northwest Austin we built two, with no hardware in view.
Can a fire pit go next to a water feature?
Yes. In Southeast Austin a curved steel fire pit follows the front of a round pool, and the flames reflect on the water. In Pearson Ranch in Northwest Austin a floating fire pit sits in the same bed as two boulder fountains.
Does a water feature need electricity?
Yes. Every water feature has a pump, and the pump runs on a dedicated outdoor GFCI circuit. We plan the power with the plumbing, before any concrete is poured.
Can a water feature be added to a patio I already have?
Yes. In Pearson Ranch in Northwest Austin we expanded an existing patio and added two drilled boulder fountains and a floating fire pit beside it.
What does a water feature cost in Austin?
Every water feature is priced after a free on-site consultation. The type, the size, the rock in the ground, and the distance to power drive the price. Our water feature cost guide lists the ranges by type.
